# Feature: Wind accuracy

## What it does

Makes the wind rose, the plan wind maps and the 3D wind closer to what wind consultants use for the same site. Kelvin approved four fixes on 18 Nov 2026 after the Pyrmont check (Riverview's short 7-year file, the Airport being the consultants' station, and the three regional design winds):

1. **Longest weather record.** Use the longest weather record available for each station, not the file whose name says 2009–2023. Riverview Observatory, for example, goes from 7 years (2009–2015) to 19 years (1997–2015).
2. **Sydney Airport for harbour sites.** Prefer Sydney Airport for harbour sites, and say in the wind panel which station is used, how far away it is, the years covered, and why a closer station was skipped.
3. **Design winds.** Add the three design winds, north-east, south and west, to the maps alongside the seasons.
4. **Faster wind over water.** Wind runs faster over open water than over streets and buildings. Superseded on 19 Nov 2026: the plan maps now leave water unpainted (see Kelvin's 19 Nov call below).

Kelvin's call on 19 Nov 2026, after comparing the Pyrmont Comfort map with his White Bay CFD reference: ignore water on the plan maps, and give buildings better wakes, windward-corner speed-ups and gap and street channelling.

All of this stays indicative, not a CFD study.

## Sources

- Harbour sites use the Sydney Airport anemometer, as in the Pyrmont and Blackwattle Bay pedestrian wind studies: Windtech WD758-22F01 (June 2022), Windtech WD758-25F02 (Nov 2023), CPP Project 9079 (Jan 2020) and CPP City of Sydney Project 7224 (June 2013).
- The NE, S and W design winds follow the three Sydney Airport design winds assessed in Windtech WD758-22F01 and CPP Project 9079.
- Over open water the power-law exponent is α = 0.11, the near-neutral value measured at sea by Hsu, Meindl and Gilhousen (1994), "Determining the power-law wind-profile exponent under near-neutral stability conditions at sea", Journal of Applied Meteorology 33(6).

Note after fix 1 (18 Nov 2026): Observatory Hill's full record (1954–2025, 64 years) passes the calm checks (5.4% calm in winter, 6.7% over the year). Its 2009–2023 file failed them (99.9% calm in winter). Until fix 2, Pyrmont therefore uses Observatory Hill, 2.0 km away, instead of Riverview. Fix 2 still prefers Sydney Airport for harbour sites, so the panel has to say why Observatory Hill was skipped.

Melbourne Regional Office shows the same pattern. Its full record (1954–2015, 54 years) passes the calm checks (11.5% calm in winter), so Melbourne CBD now uses it, 0.9 km away, instead of Olympic Park (2.8 km). The station closed in 2015. 17 stations now pass the calm checks that failed before; none went the other way. The station list grows from 796 to 875, because full-record files were never listed before.

Note after fix 2 (18 Nov 2026): sites within 12 km of Fort Denison count as harbour sites and use Sydney Airport (9.0 km from Pyrmont, 1940–2025, 82 years) when its record passes the calm checks. That covers Pyrmont, the CBD, North Sydney, Balmain, Rozelle and Mosman, but not Parramatta, Penrith or Hornsby. Everywhere else still uses the nearest station that passes the calm checks. For Pyrmont the panel lists the closer stations it skipped: Observatory Hill (2.0 km), Riverview (4.8 km) and Canterbury Park Racecourse (7.9 km), all for "harbour site: Sydney Airport preferred". A station that fails the calm checks is listed as "wind record mostly calm, N% calm in winter".

Note on gappy long records (18 Nov 2026): Sydney Airport's full record (1940–2025) builds its typical months from the 1950s and 60s, which look like 3-hourly observations with the gaps read as calm: 10.8% calm over the year, 16.8% in summer and 20.2% in winter, against 1–3% in every season in each of its 15-year files and in BoM's 3 pm roses. That lowered the speeds (5.1 instead of 5.8 m/s over the year) and turned summer north-east. A longer record is now used only if its calm share, over the year and in every season, is no more than 5 points above the station's most recent dated file, when that file itself passes the calm checks. Otherwise the next longest is tried, and between equal lengths the later one. Sydney Airport now uses 2011–2025 (15 years): 1.6% calm over the year, summer from the south at 6.2 m/s. Riverview (1997–2015) and Observatory Hill (1954–2025, whose recent file is mostly calm) keep their full records, as do Melbourne Regional Office and Viewbank. 155 of 875 stations change record this way.

Note after fix 3 (18 Nov 2026): design winds sit next to the season buttons on the wind console. Sites that use Sydney Airport get the consultants' north-east, south and west (45°, 180°, 270°). Everywhere else gets the three of the eight compass directions whose winds bring the most energy over the year (hours × speed³ within 22.5° either side), at least 67.5° apart. Each design wind's speed is the average station speed of the year's hours within 22.5° of its heading, brought down to walking height the same way as the seasons. Pyrmont: NE 6.2 m/s at the station, 3.9 m/s at walking height, 18% of hours; S 7.5 and 4.6 m/s, 23%; W 5.5 and 3.4 m/s, 22%. Mont Albert North (Viewbank): N, SE and SW. Comfort still uses all winds over the year when a design wind is picked.

Note after fix 4 (18 Nov 2026): the plan maps treat the model's water areas, the same ones the plan draws as water (NSW HydroArea for Pyrmont), as open water. Over open water the surface is smoother, so wind near the surface keeps more of its speed: the station speed comes down to walking height with a power-law exponent of 0.11 instead of 0.25, a factor of 0.81 instead of 0.62, so open water runs about 1.3 times faster than open land. Land within 60 m of water gets part of the boost, falling off evenly with distance. Buildings over water stay solid. Speed and Comfort use the same factor, and the card adds "Faster over water (smoother surface)" when the site has water. The Reference row stays the land speed. The 3D ground wind is coloured relative to open ground, so it does not change. Pyrmont, design wind W: open water in the bays runs at about 4.5 m/s, against 3.4 m/s on open land. Mont Albert North has no water areas and is unchanged.

Note on the speed scale (18 Nov 2026): the Wind speed map now fits its colour scale to each site instead of a fixed 0–4 m/s, so Pyrmont is no longer clipped at 4 m/s (design wind S alone runs about 6 m/s over open water). The top is a round number at or above the fastest speed the map paints in any of the site's eight views (Annual, the four seasons and the three design winds), never below 4 m/s, and it stays the same when switching between those views. Comfort keeps its fixed Lawson thresholds. Kelvin also accepted the Melbourne Regional Office switch: the station closed in 2015, and Melbourne CBD uses it at 0.9 km instead of Olympic Park. Station picking is unchanged.

Note on scale stability (19 Nov 2026): the same Pyrmont cut showed a 0–8 top in one load (11 of 21 sources) and 0–10 in another (21 of 22). The top was set by the single fastest painted cell across the eight views. Pyrmont's strongest view, Spring from the south, runs at 5.5 m/s on open land and about 7.1 m/s over open water, so one corner or gap speed-up 14% above open water anywhere on the map pushed the top past 8.1 and on to 10. Buildings, trees or water arriving in a later or fuller load add or move those speed-ups, so the top flipped between loads. Results were never shared between two loads (the cache was per model), so it was the single-cell rule, not a stale cache. The top now comes from the 99.5th percentile of the painted walking-height speeds over open (non-building) cells in each view, the largest of those across the eight views, with the same round-number rule (4 or less gives 4; 4 to 6 rounds up to the next whole m/s; above 6 rounds up to the next even number). The scale is cached by a fingerprint of the obstacle heights, tree canopy and water mask the map is drawn from, so a partial site never reuses a complete one, and the same complete site always gets the same top however many times it is loaded. The map waits for that top before colouring, as before. Picking a direction on the rose never changes it. Expected Pyrmont top: 8 m/s (open water in Spring from the south about 7.1 m/s; the 99.5th percentile would need to sit 14% above open water to reach 10). This is worked out from the station data and still needs a check on the live site.

On a 0–10 scale, design wind NE (3.9 m/s on land, about 5.1 m/s over open water) sat in one narrow mid-cyan band, so the shore blend and the wakes over water were hard to see. The field itself already has them: the water uses the same flow model as land, so buildings on the shore leave slower wakes over the water, and the 60 m shore blend runs from the land speed to the water speed. A test now checks both. On a 0–8 scale the same speeds span cyan to green-yellow. No extra water physics was added. (Superseded by the 19 Nov call below: water is no longer painted.)

Note on Kelvin's 19 Nov 2026 call (water and wakes): the plan maps no longer paint water. Water cells come from the model's water areas (the same mask as fix 4; buildings over water stay solid and still draw as footprints). On Wind speed and Comfort the cut shows the muted grey base map first, and the speed or comfort colour is painted only on land, so water shows as the base map's grey-blue water. Water cells are left out of the 99.5th-percentile scale fit and out of the Unsafe count, so a windy bay cannot lift the top or add an Unsafe area. Land within 60 m of water keeps the quiet shore boost (the 0.11 to 0.25 exponent blend), so streets at the water's edge can run a little faster than inland. The card line "Faster over water (smoother surface)" is gone. Pyrmont's top is now set by land, not open water, so it may come down from 8 m/s; that still needs a check on the live site.

The per-direction flow field behind both maps and the 3D ground wind also changes. Building wakes reach 4H (up to 240 m, was 3H capped at 90 m, so towers over 30 m got a stub) and still ease off with distance (the floor + (1 − floor)·t² shape). Each wake widens slowly downwind into a tapered streak (about 0.12 m sideways per metre, up to 16 m each side) rather than a one-cell ray. Each building's two windward corners, where the flow separates, get an acceleration zone up to 1.7× the local speed that runs downwind along the side and fades over about 1.5H (16–48 m); the plain side boost dropped from 0.3 to 0.2 so the corner reads clearly. Gaps and streets with buildings on both sides now channel wind out to 40 m either side (was 24 m), so wind-aligned streets up to about 60 m wide speed up. Trees keep their shorter 1.5H wake. Wind speed and a picked design wind use one heading; Comfort still combines every heading by the station's direction frequencies. Lawson thresholds and the Weibull tail are unchanged. This is still a rule-based, indicative field, not CFD.

Kelvin rejected the 11:42 AM lighter-grey and over-water trials at 12:17 PM AEDT on 19 Nov 2026: keep the arrows black and on land only. Each arrow's direction is the per-heading flow vector from the same field as the wakes, corners and channels above, lightly smoothed, so arrows bend around buildings. Wind speed and a picked rose sector or design wind use that one heading; Comfort uses the frequency-weighted vector sum of every heading's flow (each heading weighted by its share of the season's wind times its mean station speed, the same season weights that build Comfort), so its arrows show the mean local flow and still deflect. Length scales with walking-height speed between 72% and 100% of an 18px cap; shaft and head are sized in screen pixels (1px shaft, 2px head, `#1A1A1A` at 70% opacity). Zoom-out thinning still drops every second (then fourth) row and column when neighbours would sit closer than 7px, so the 22 m grid still shows at a 1–1.5 km plan view and further zoom-out still thins. A default-on Arrows toggle sits beside the Wind speed | Comfort switch. No new solver: the arrows reuse the cached per-heading grids. Still indicative, not CFD.

## Out of scope

- No CFD or wind-tunnel modelling.
- No gust or 5% exceedance statistics.
- No live or forecast weather.
